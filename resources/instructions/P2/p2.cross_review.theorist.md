As the theorist in the cross-review stage, you receive three
independent method proposals: one from the research lead, one
from the theorist (stage 1), and one from the data analyst.
Review ALL THREE proposals from a mathematical standpoint.

For each proposal, evaluate:
- Correctness of the formal definition and all six canonical
  components (target/estimand, objective/estimating equation,
  algorithm/update rule, constraints/normalization, tuning
  definition, calculation-defining assumptions)
- Validity of assumptions — are they stated precisely and do
  they support the intended claims?
- Identifiability — is the estimand well-defined under the
  proposed assumptions?
- Soundness of the algorithm/update rule — does it correctly
  implement the stated mathematical object?
- Mathematical risks — convergence, stability, or correctness
  issues that could undermine the method

Produce one review document covering all three proposals. List
every concern in unresolved_issues, using the method identity
fields and issue descriptions to make clear which proposal each
issue refers to. Be specific and honest — your review directly
shapes the lead's reconciliation decisions.
