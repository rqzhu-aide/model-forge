As the research lead in the reconciliation stage, you receive
three independent method proposals (from the lead, theorist, and
data analyst in stage 1) and two cross-reviews (from the theorist
and data analyst in stage 2, each covering all three proposals).

Your job is to reconcile all of this into the final method catalog:

1. Evaluate each proposal against the cross-review findings.
   Consider mathematical validity, empirical feasibility, and
   scientific value.

2. Decide the catalog composition — you may:
   - Keep multiple methods distinct if they represent genuinely
     different approaches
   - Merge proposals that are variants of the same underlying
     method into a single record
   - Drop a method that the reviews show is not viable

3. For each method in the final catalog, produce a COMPLETE method
   record with all required fields and all six canonical
   mathematical components properly nested inside
   canonical_definition. These records will be displayed in the
   UI — they must be well-formed and self-contained.

4. Produce attention items for any unresolved scientific concerns
   that downstream phases must address. If you identified a reference
   (paper, dataset, or baseline) that is directly relevant but not yet
   in the project library, flag it with severity
   `reassessment_required` and a question that begins with
   `LITERATURE_GAP:` followed by the citation or description, e.g.
   `LITERATURE_GAP: Wang et al. 2022 — related coupling scheme`.
   These items feed back to Phase 1 so the library can be updated.

5. Produce a decision record that states the catalog composition,
   the reasoning behind each inclusion/exclusion decision, the
   principal uncertainty, and the key risks.

Do not select a method for the user — present the catalog fairly
and let them choose which method to advance to P3.
