# Contribution Boundary Analysis

A contribution claim is only as strong as its boundary: the exact point where
prior work ends and this work begins. Vague novelty ("we propose a new
method") invites reviewer skepticism; an explicit boundary invites engagement.

## Procedure

1. **Name the closest credible alternatives.** Identify the two to four works
   a skeptical reader would raise first. If you cannot name them, the
   literature basis is incomplete — say so rather than writing around it.
2. **State the shared core.** One sentence: what this work and the closest
   alternatives have in common (problem, object of study, or technique).
3. **State the delta precisely.** For each alternative, one sentence: what
   changes here (assumption, regime, guarantee, cost model, scope). Deltas
   must be checkable against the frozen record, not stylistic ("more
   principled", "more efficient" without a cost statement).
4. **Classify each delta.** Novel combination, new assumption regime, new
   guarantee, new empirical finding, new negative result, or correction of
   prior work. Negative results and boundary clarifications are legitimate
   contributions — label them as what they are.
5. **Mark the edge of support.** For every delta, name the evidence that
   supports it (theorem, experiment, derivation) and where that support
   stops. A delta supported only by intuition is a conjecture — write it as
   one.

## Failure modes to reject

- Novelty by unfamiliarity: the delta exists in literature you have not
  checked. Downgrade the claim or cite the gap honestly.
- Boundary by omission: ignoring the strongest alternative because it
  weakens the claim. Address it in the limitations instead.
- Claim inflation in the abstract: every abstract-level novelty phrase must
  survive the delta table above.

## Output discipline

Keep a short delta table (alternative / shared core / delta / support) in
your working notes and mirror its conclusions in the manuscript's
positioning paragraphs. The claim traceability record cites the same
evidence objects; the two must agree.
