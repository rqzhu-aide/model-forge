# Review Calibration

A useful review is calibrated: every finding carries the severity its
consequence deserves, cites the evidence that supports it, and separates
what the science needs from what the reviewer prefers.

## Severity calibration

- **Blocking**: the manuscript's central claim is unsupported, incorrect, or
  untraceable. Examples: a theorem whose proof has a gap in the main
  argument; headline numbers that cannot be regenerated from the recorded
  artifacts; a contribution claim contradicted by cited prior work. Ask:
  "if this is not fixed, is the paper's conclusion wrong or unknowable?"
  Only then is it blocking.
- **Major**: a material claim is under-supported, a key baseline or
  ablation is missing, an assumption is unstated but load-bearing, or the
  writing obscures the argument in a section central to the contribution.
  The paper could be right, but the record does not show it yet.
- **Minor**: presentation, notation, terminology, local clarity, optional
  robustness checks. Never inflate a taste preference into a major finding.

## Finding discipline

- Every finding cites its evidence: the exact section, equation, table, or
  artifact it refers to, and what you checked. "The proof seems incomplete"
  is unusable; "Theorem 2, step 3 applies dominated convergence without
  verifying uniform integrability" is a finding.
- Distinguish the manuscript's defects from the field's disagreements. A
  method choice you would not have made is a comment, not a finding, unless
  you can name the concrete failure it causes.
- State the smallest resolution that would close the finding. If you cannot
  say what would close it, the finding is not ready.

## Verdict discipline

- The verdict follows the findings, never precedes them. Count the blocking
  findings first; the verdict is their consequence.
- Say what the manuscript does well with the same precision you apply to
  its defects. A review that finds nothing to praise has usually not read
  for understanding.
- Declare your uncertainty: if you could not verify a proof or rerun an
  experiment in the time available, say so explicitly rather than
  softening a finding you did not check.
