# Literature Synthesis

A literature synthesis is not an annotated bibliography. It is an argument:
the field believes X on the strength of Y, the argument has a gap at Z, and
that gap is exactly what this project addresses. Every part of that argument
must trace to specific sources.

## Procedure

1. **Cluster the library by claim, not by paper.** Two papers that establish
   the same result belong to one cluster; a paper can appear in several
   clusters. Each cluster gets a one-line claim statement.
2. **Rate the strength of each cluster.** Distinguish: proved (formal
   result), demonstrated (replicated empirical evidence), argued (plausible
   mechanism, weak evidence), asserted (folklore). The rating decides what
   the synthesis may lean on.
3. **Build the coverage matrix.** Rows: the sub-questions the research
   question decomposes into. Columns: clusters. A cell marks whether the
   cluster answers, partially answers, or ignores the sub-question. Empty
   rows are candidate gaps; empty columns mean the cluster is irrelevant
   (drop it from the synthesis, keep it in the library).
4. **State gaps as falsifiable absences.** "Little work on X" is not a gap.
   A gap names the specific question, the closest existing work, and why
   that work does not answer it (different regime, different estimand,
   missing guarantee). If the closest work does answer it, there is no gap.
5. **Position, do not disparage.** The synthesis earns the project's
   existence by precision about what exists, not by diminishing it. Every
   claim about prior work must be one the cited authors would recognize as
   fair.

## Coverage discipline

Coverage is a claim and must be auditable. Record the search query, the
databases, the date, and the inclusion rule. A synthesis that cannot say how
its library was assembled is an opinion piece. Mark every sub-question whose
coverage rests on fewer than three independent sources as thin.

## Output discipline

The synthesis record separates: established ground (citable, load-bearing),
contested ground (name the sides), and open ground (the gap statements).
Never let a gap statement rest on a source the library does not contain.
